Adaptive gear for wheelchair pickleball

A spare ball, clipped to your chair.

PickleDock clamps onto the frame of a sports wheelchair and holds an extra pickleball right where your hand already is. Pull it out, serve, and keep playing.

Why it exists

Your hands are already busy.

In wheelchair pickleball you push the chair and swing the paddle with the same hands. A spare ball in your lap tends to roll off, and pockets are hard to reach while seated.

PickleDock gives the ball a fixed home on the side of the chair, so grabbing it is one short reach.

How it works

Two parts, one minute to set up.

  1. 1

    Snap the clamp on

    The C-shaped clamp pushes onto a round frame tube. Pick the size that matches your chair.

  2. 2

    Slide the cup on

    A tapered dovetail rail on the clamp fits a slot under the cup. It wedges tighter the further it goes.

  3. 3

    Drop a ball in

    Four flexible fingers open around the ball and click shut past a small lip. A light pull gets it back out.

The story so far

It started at a ping pong table.

  1. Ping pong balls kept rolling away

    The first version was a small holder glued to a ping pong paddle, built to stop balls from blowing off the table.

  2. From the table to the court

    The clamp idea turned out to fit wheelchair rails. After learning about adaptive sports through the Challenged Athletes Foundation, the project shifted to wheelchair pickleball.

  3. First 3D printed docks

    A ball cage on a screw-on clamp. It held, but it was hard to put on and take off, and tough to print cleanly.

  4. Version 2: rebuilt to print without supports

    The cup now sits flat on the printer bed, with thicker fingers and a slide-on dovetail in place of the screw.

  5. Version 6: a cleaner clamp

    After v2 the clamp went through a few more rounds. v6 keeps the proven v2 cup and adds a taller clamp with a round face that matches the cup.

  6. Tested with athletes

    Fit and strength both passed at the October 2026 session in San Diego. Placement is the next thing to work on.

Assembly tips

  • Slide the cup on from the wide end of the rail until it wedges.
  • On an upright tube, point the narrow end of the rail down so gravity keeps it tight.
  • Your slicer may flag the slot roof as a "floating cantilever." It's a short bridge and prints fine.
  • Add a drop of glue if you want it permanent.
